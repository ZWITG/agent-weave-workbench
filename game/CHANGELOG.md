# Relivable: major updates

Named updates since the game was created (26 October 2026). Newest last.

## 1.0 "First Breath" (26 Oct)
- The first playable Flutter version of Relivable, with its dark "Night Feed" look.
- A Windows desktop build and installer.
- Money shown in the header, and swipe up to open Activities (45 activities).
- Relationships with bond, memories and grudges.
- Original sound effects.

## 1.1 "Playtime" (27 Oct)
- The first activity mini-games, and options on the Path tab.
- Results shown as pop-ups.
- The Wealth tab economy.
- Auto-updates on Android phones.

## 1.2 "Living World" (27 Oct)
- World events and a newspaper.
- People in your life get their own news.
- Richer event writing.

## 1.3 "Whole Life" (the Waves) (27 Oct)
- **A:** a full lifespan, birthday summaries, Rewind and settings.
- **B:** school.
- **C:** deeper careers.
- **D:** love, children and dynasties.
- **E:** money, homes, crime and health.
- **F:** identity, countries, fame, social media, pets, achievements, goals and accessibility.
- **G:**
  - heists, lawsuits and prison riots;
  - funerals and Life Recap cards;
  - heirlooms, collectibles, boats, planes and disasters;
  - fostering, surrogacy, grandchildren, the seasons and 61 new stories.

## 1.4 "Pocket & Shop" (27–30 Oct)
- iPhone and iPad version, plus a home-screen web app.
- A proper shop.
- Every tab made to work like Activities.

## 1.5 "Smooth Sailing" (30 Oct)
- Events flow one at a time.
- Every screen fits small phones and large text.
- More choices in activities, events and people.

## 1.6 "Behind Bars" (30 Oct)
- Prison gangs, visits, yard trading, courses, solitary confinement, new escapes and life after release.
- Crimes as planned jobs, with a target, an approach and twists.
- Street skill and lying low.
- Murder.
- Keeping cars you steal.

## 1.7 "Headlines" (30 Oct)
- A big-news pop-up after each birthday.
- The odds of everything hidden from the player.
- News that affects your life and doesn't repeat itself.
- Friends you make join People.

## 1.8 "Game Night" (30 Oct)
- 8 new mini-games, dressed as each activity.
- Redo everyday activities up to three times a year, with smaller gains each time.

## 1.9 "Big Screen" (30 Oct)
- A desktop layout with tabs down the side and Activities docked on the right.
- Everything you own shown together on the Wealth tab.

## 2.0 "Home & Hustle" (31 Oct)
- 90 more shop items and two new departments.
- Landlord portfolios, moving house, remortgaging and finding tenants.
- Cards for things you own expand to show their options.
- Tap your money to open Wealth.

## 2.1 "Spotlight" (31 Oct)
- Fame reworked, with public image, fame tiers and celebrity life.
- A fame badge under your money.
- Social media moved into Activities.

## 2.2 "Passport" (31 Oct)
- 10 new countries, with researched living conditions.
- 17 new sound effects.
- Moving abroad uses a drop-down list.

## 2.3 "Road to the Underground" (31 Oct)
- A music career: write and release songs (and name them yourself).
- Songwriting is a skill, and hits depend on quality.
- Fame from music once you're established.

## 2.4 "Upper Echelon" (31 Oct)
- Money in local currencies.
- A hidden stage at US$100M with islands, yachts, sports teams, space flights, a billionaire milestone and secret achievements.

## 2.5 "Empire" (31 Oct)
- Run up to five businesses, each with its own trade, upgrades, signature move and events.
- Drop-down lists across the game.

## 2.6 "Clean Sweep" (31 Oct)
- An automated player that plays thousands of lives to find logic errors.
- Fixed: exes deleted from your People, stale "married" status, keeping your job in prison, and more.

## 2.7 "Family Matters" (31 Oct)
- Surprise pregnancies, birth plans, varied births, twins and triplets, and placing a baby for adoption with a reunion later.
- Funerals and wills, honeymoons, being fired, grandchildren, step-parents.
- Grief and other big moments now last for years.
- Teen pregnancy removed.

## 2.8 "Your Way" (31 Oct)
- 18 everyday activities now ask how you want to do them (diets, nights out, tattoos, raises, tours and more).
- Realistic lottery odds.

## 2.9 "Underworld" (31 Oct)
- Heat you can see and manage.
- Dirty money and six ways to launder it.
- Three street crews with ranks, informing and takeovers.
- 7 new crimes.
- A friendlier Crime tab grouped into sections.

## 2.10 "Curtain Up" (31 Oct)
- An opening animation when the game starts or is opened again: the ZWITG mark (letters settle, the red line draws across), then the Relivable logo, then into the game.
- Tap to skip; shorter and calmer with Reduce motion on.
- Shareable intro videos in `media/` (square 1080×1080 and vertical 1080×1920), rebuilt with `tool/make_intro_video.sh`.

## 2.11 "Tidy Up" (31 Oct)
- The Identity, Memories and Collections tiles are hidden from the Me tab.
- Rewind is now a small button beside Age +1 (orange when a rewind is available, grey when not; hidden for Ironman lives) instead of a Me tab tile.
- On very narrow phones the search box shrinks to its icon so everything fits.

## 2.12 "No Papers" (31 Oct)
- Cross into another country without a visa: overstay as a tourist (safest), pay a smuggler (could be a scam) or hide in a lorry or boat (cheap and dangerous).
- Life without papers: only cash-in-hand jobs at lower pay, no bank loans, and immigration checks where you can run, claim asylum or go home.
- A criminal conviction means deportation; a deportation on file makes future visas harder.
- Get legal: apply for papers (strong through marriage, fair after years of work) or wait for an amnesty after five years.

## 2.13 "Joined Up" (31 Oct)
- No papers, no free public healthcare; the mortgage refusal now says why.
- Famous people's convictions hit their image and followers.
- Your crew looks after you in prison (and word gets out about informants); moving abroad or being deported ends your crew membership.
- Funerals while you are in prison: ask for day release, write a letter, or grieve alone.
- Businesses slip while their owner is in prison; crews can lean on business owners for protection money.
- The big-news pop-up now catches deportations, amnesties, asylum, crew promotions, billionaire news and grandchildren.

## 2.14 "Living History" (31 Oct)
- New world news that changes play: border crackdowns, amnesties, police crackdowns on organised crime, tourist booms and market frenzies.
- The world reaches the newer systems: amnesties give papers; crackdowns make crossings and checks riskier; policing raises suspicion of dirty money; crew rackets follow the economy; tourists fill cafés; a pandemic empties restaurants, cancels tours and favours apps; market frenzies boost insider trading and crypto.
- An infamous family name gets you noticed by crews.
- Your biggest moves go into the world's history and your family's reputation: billionaires, the super-rich, foundations, space flights, crime bosses, secret witnesses, business empires, local music stars and deportations.

## 2.15 "Yard Time" (31 Oct)
- The whole game changes look while you are in prison: institutional green with chalk-mint accents, and back to Night Feed the day you are released.
- Decals: a chain-link fence along the top, a padlock on the year, a chalk tally card ("2 years down, 3 to go") with your release year, parole countdown and a stencilled block tag, a chalk sun, a barbed-wire divider, and stencil stamps on prison stories (YARD, VISIT, LETTER, COURSE, WORK, FIGHT, GANG, PAROLE, SOLITARY).
- Prison values replace outside ones: Behaviour and Respect instead of Smarts and Looks, your stash instead of your bank balance, your prison job and prison instead of job and city, and "Serve a year" instead of Age +1.

## 2.16 "Inside Out" (31 Oct)
- Prison is properly separate from life outside: proposals, weddings, honeymoons, family planning, seasonal outings, fostering, record deals, award shows, the military draft, business events, crew business and island or team purchases wait until you are out. Everyday country life, galas and invitations pause, and relationships do not move forward through the glass.
- News about people reaches you at visiting time, not at the kitchen table.
- People you do not see drift while you are inside (children most); visits keep them close.
- Your child's wedding and a new grandchild have prison versions (a speech read for you, money, a letter, a family visit).
- A terminal diagnosis inside lets you apply for compassionate release.
- Family looks after your pets while you are inside, or they are rehomed; youth detention pauses school life.
- Release day: choose how to spend your first night out (home to family, celebrating with old friends, or with your old cellmate). Your prison stash stays inside.

## 2.17 "Behind the Walls" (31 Oct)
- Illness, check-ups and mental health have prison versions: the medical list, a guarded hospital transfer, resting in your cell, the prison phone, the counsellor and programme groups (which count towards parole). Care is free inside.
- Deaths reach you through the prison chaplain; heirlooms are kept for you; grandchildren's milestones and your children's news come by letter; nudges say "Write to them?"
- Celebrity life (parties, paparazzi, brand gifts, rumours, stalkers), restaurant reactions to your family name and outside-world quiet years pause or change inside.

## 2.18 "Lockdown" (31 Oct)
- Going to prison is a moment: the screen freezes, a siren washes over it, cell bars slam down with a clang and a shake, and Yard Time green creeps out of every bar and eats the old look away until it is gone. "BOOKED", your inmate number and your sentence are stamped across it, and the bars lift into the fence. Tap to skip; Reduce motion makes it a short fade.
- Release plays it in reverse: the gate rattles open and the night colours come back through the gap, stamped "RELEASED" (or "ESCAPED").
- Every tab becomes a prison place while you are inside, with stencilled tab names: Cell (Life), Visits (your cellmate, your crew and its rivals, the visiting list and who is banned), File (your sentence, parole, prison job, courses and the ways out), Canteen (your stash, the yard's going rates on a chalkboard, and your outside money held at reception) and ID (an inmate card with a mugshot, height chart and number).
- Activities becomes The Yard: prison activities laid out by place (yard, work and classes, contact, crew, ways out, quiet time), with outside activities out of sight until release.
- Working out in the yard and exercising in your cell are now mini-games (pull-ups, the punch bag, push-ups, the plank), played against a chain-link yard or behind cell bars. A strong set in the yard earns respect.

## 2.19 "The Yard" (31 Oct)
- Activities inside is a map of the prison: the cell block, workshop, weights pit, basketball court, visiting room and gate, drawn from above with a guard tower sweeping its searchlight across the yard. Tap a place to jump to what you can do there; places with nothing open are greyed out (in solitary, everything but your cell).
- Results inside come back as a stamped block report, and option menus are stencilled ("Pick your move").
- Your cell: buy things for your shelf with your stash. A radio, paperbacks, photos and a hot plate help a little every year. A smuggled phone, a shank and a bag of brew are contraband: they do more (calls home, winning yard fights, a happier year), but cell searches can find them, and a shank found costs a year and solitary. Your shelf is on the Canteen tab.
- The yard gives you a nickname once people know you, from what you are known for: Bench, Professor, Chef, Books, Keys, Iron, Owl, Accountant, DJ, Boss, or Canary if you snitched. It goes on your inmate ID.

## 2.20 "The Echelon" (31 Oct)
Life at the top rebuilt around goals money alone does not settle. Players of life sims say the late game goes quiet once you have bought the biggest house, so the upper echelon now has a ladder to climb, rivals to beat, power to use and a legacy to leave.
- The Invitation: crossing $100 million plays a moment. The screen goes dark, a black envelope edged in gold rises, a gold wax seal stamps it and cracks, and an engraved card slides out: "You are cordially invited to the Upper Echelon". Each tier after that gets a gold plaque and a burst of sparks.
- The Echelon: its own screen in obsidian and gold leaf with a serif face (Playfair Display), at the top of the Wealth tab and the Upper echelon activities.
- Six tiers: Centimillionaire, Half-billionaire, Billionaire, Decabillionaire, Centibillionaire and Richest Alive, each unlocking bigger plays, with a ladder showing how far it is to the next.
- The List: eight named rival tycoons (rockets, luxury fashion, chips and AI, old oil money, pharma, a hedge fund...) whose fortunes rise and fall every year. Climb past them, feud with them, and watch them age and hand over to heirs who lose some of it. Rivals make moves: bidding against you at auction, offering for your team, proposing joint ventures, challenging you to out-give them, and suing you once a feud boils over.
- Influence (0–100), earned from your newspaper, foundation, politics and rank, and spent on power moves: lobbying for a tax break, killing a story, hosting a summit, a hostile takeover of a rival, and making a head of government.
- Moonshots from $10 billion: fusion power, a Mars outpost, curing a disease, cleaning the oceans or an AI lab. Years of funding and a real chance it fails; success puts your name in the history books.
- Heirs: most billionaire heirs lose a comma because nobody prepared them (UBS/PwC). Prepare your heirs with board seats, time working elsewhere and family councils. A ready heir keeps the family office, foundation and seat on the List; an unprepared one loses a quarter to lawyers and hangers-on.
- At $100 billion: buy a social network or found a city with your name on it.
- A trophy cabinet, a legacy score and every upper-echelon purchase grouped by what it is for.
- Fortunes over $10 million show short in the header ($160M), in gold once you are in the upper echelon.
