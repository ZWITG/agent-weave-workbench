## 2.18 "Lockdown" (31 Oct)
- Going to prison is a moment: the screen freezes, a siren washes over it, cell bars slam down with a clang and a shake, and Yard Time green creeps out of every bar and eats the old look away until it is gone. "BOOKED", your inmate number and your sentence are stamped across it, and the bars lift into the fence. Tap to skip; Reduce motion makes it a short fade.
- Release plays it in reverse: the gate rattles open and the night colours come back through the gap, stamped "RELEASED" (or "ESCAPED").
- Every tab becomes a prison place while you are inside, with stencilled tab names: Cell (Life), Visits (your cellmate, your crew and its rivals, the visiting list and who is banned), File (your sentence, parole, prison job, courses and the ways out), Canteen (your stash, the yard's going rates on a chalkboard, and your outside money held at reception) and ID (an inmate card with a mugshot, height chart and number).
- Activities becomes The Yard: prison activities laid out by place (yard, work and classes, contact, crew, ways out, quiet time), with outside activities out of sight until release.
- Working out in the yard and exercising in your cell are now mini-games (pull-ups, the punch bag, push-ups, the plank), played against a chain-link yard or behind cell bars. A strong set in the yard earns respect.

## 2.19 "The Yard" (31 Oct)
- Activities inside is a map of the prison: the cell block, workshop, weights pit, basketball court, visiting room and gate, drawn from above with a guard tower sweeping its searchlight across the yard. Tap a place to jump to what you can do there; places with nothing open are greyed out (in solitary, everything but your cell).
- Results inside come back as a stamped block report, and option menus are stencilled ("Pick your move").
- Your cell: buy things for your shelf with your stash. A radio, paperbacks, photos and a hot plate help a little every year. A smuggled phone, a shank and a bag of brew are contraband: they do more (calls home, winning yard fights, a happier year), but cell searches can find them, and a shank found costs a year and solitary. Your shelf is on the Canteen tab.
- The yard gives you a nickname once people know you, from what you are known for: Bench, Professor, Chef, Books, Keys, Iron, Owl, Accountant, DJ, Boss, or Canary if you snitched. It goes on your inmate ID.

## 2.20 "The Echelon" (31 Oct)
Life at the top rebuilt around goals money alone does not settle. Players of life sims say the late game goes quiet once you have bought the biggest house, so the upper echelon now has a ladder to climb, rivals to beat, power to use and a legacy to leave.
- The Invitation: crossing $100 million plays a moment. The screen goes dark, a black envelope edged in gold rises, a gold wax seal stamps it and cracks, and an engraved card slides out: "You are cordially invited to the Upper Echelon". Each tier after that gets a gold plaque and a burst of sparks.
- The Echelon: its own screen in obsidian and gold leaf with a serif face (Playfair Display), at the top of the Wealth tab and the Upper echelon activities.
- Six tiers: Centimillionaire, Half-billionaire, Billionaire, Decabillionaire, Centibillionaire and Richest Alive, each unlocking bigger plays, with a ladder showing how far it is to the next.
- The List: eight named rival tycoons (rockets, luxury fashion, chips and AI, old oil money, pharma, a hedge fund...) whose fortunes rise and fall every year. Climb past them, feud with them, and watch them age and hand over to heirs who lose some of it. Rivals make moves: bidding against you at auction, offering for your team, proposing joint ventures, challenging you to out-give them, and suing you once a feud boils over.
- Influence (0–100), earned from your newspaper, foundation, politics and rank, and spent on power moves: lobbying for a tax break, killing a story, hosting a summit, a hostile takeover of a rival, and making a head of government.
- Moonshots from $10 billion: fusion power, a Mars outpost, curing a disease, cleaning the oceans or an AI lab. Years of funding and a real chance it fails; success puts your name in the history books.
- Heirs: most billionaire heirs lose a comma because nobody prepared them (UBS/PwC). Prepare your heirs with board seats, time working elsewhere and family councils. A ready heir keeps the family office, foundation and seat on the List; an unprepared one loses a quarter to lawyers and hangers-on.
- At $100 billion: buy a social network or found a city with your name on it.
- A trophy cabinet, a legacy score and every upper-echelon purchase grouped by what it is for.
- Fortunes over $10 million show short in the header ($160M), in gold once you are in the upper echelon.

## "Career Looks" and the law at work (13 Nov)
The groundwork for the special careers update: each special career now changes how the game looks, and crime and the police are wired into work.
- Career looks: while you work in a special career the whole game wears its colours. Marquee (actor), Backstage (musician), Floodlights (athlete), Runway (model), Chamber (politician), Field Kit (armed forces), Mahogany (law), Night Shift (medicine), Flight Deck (pilot), Meadow (vet), Market Day (the street) and Blue Line (police). Every look keeps text readable (tested contrast).
- Starting one plays the Curtain: the new look opens out of the middle of the screen like a spotlight, with the career's badge, your new title and the look's name. Tap to skip; Reduce motion makes it a fade.
- Prison, the Crime and Real estate pages and the Gilded upper echelon still win over a career look. Settings › Career looks turns them off.
- Your job moves the odds of a crime, shown with the reasons: a police officer knows the patrol routes (−8), a lawyer knows how cases are built (−4), a politician in office (+6) or a well-known performer or athlete (+4) gets recognised.
- A conviction ends careers that cannot carry one: police officers are dismissed, lawyers struck off, judges removed from the bench, politicians resign; serious or violent crimes cost doctors and nurses their registration, pilots their licence, soldiers a dishonourable discharge, and teachers their job.
- Police officers: crimes you get away with go on your Internal Affairs file (crimes, planned jobs, murders and heists alike); a case you committed can land on your own desk (lose the file, pin it on someone, or work it honestly); a crew you belong to asks for favours; undercover work never sends you into your own crew; crews do not invite a cop with a clean file; and a cop on the Most Wanted list is suspended and dismissed.
- Lawyers representing themselves in court use their courtroom skill (62, plus 7 for each step up the ladder).
- A criminal record costs votes: 8 points off the election odds for each conviction on file (up to 24).
- tool/render_careers_test.dart renders the looks and the Curtain for visual checks.

## "Calling" wave 1: the Career Hub (13 Nov)

It shows the career's motif across the top (marquee bulbs, VU meters, a pitch, a ballot ribbon, a light bar), its signature meter, the season, the ladder with what the next step needs, the career's own work and what has happened so far.

- Athlete: choose a sport (football, basketball, tennis, athletics) in the fictional National League. Train (with an overtraining risk), rest, study footage and sign sponsors; every season plays out to a record and a league place, the top four play a final, and contracts come up every three years (re-sign, ask for more, or move clubs).
- Politician: give speeches, hold rallies, meet constituents, fundraise (with a donor-scandal risk) and vote on bills that voters like or dislike. Approval and the campaign fund now count at elections.
- Police: walk the beat or reopen a cold case from the Hub, alongside the academy, casework and the badge.
- The job board shows "the way in" for every special career: a checklist of what it needs and what you already have.
- The Curtain Call: a promotion in a special career plays the Curtain again with "PROMOTED TO".

## "Calling" wave 2: seven more Career Hubs (13 Nov)
Every remaining special career now has its own Hub (docs/phase-2/03, step 5): a signature meter, its own activities, a season of four beats with scenes, and promotion from the meter with a clear "what's next" on the ladder.
- Model (Runway) · Portfolio: castings (the walk, played), test shoots, fashion week, brand campaigns, a top agency, and magazine-cover offers (classic or bold). After 30 the Portfolio fades faster. Campaign model needs a campaign first.
- Doctor (Night Shift) · Ward trust: A&E triage (played), extra night shifts, research papers, teaching and aid missions. Choose a specialty (emergency medicine, cardiology or paediatrics), and decide who covers doubles in the winter crisis. Surgeons need a specialty and chiefs a published paper.
- Vet (Meadow) · Standing: the clinic (played: match signs to causes), farm calls, nights on call, free check-ups for your own pets, lambing-night calls, and opening your own practice, which earns every year and is how you become Practice owner.
- Pilot (Flight Deck) · Flight hours: fly short-haul, long-haul or cargo, staff travel, the pilots' union, and a check ride (a crosswind landing, played) for every seat. Peak season can bring an engine warning: divert or carry on.
- Armed forces (Field Kit) · Readiness: the obstacle course (played), training courses, the officer course (needed for Lieutenant), missions, mentoring recruits and leave. Night exercises on the moors; deployments now move Readiness and count medals.
- Lawyer and judge (Mahogany) · Reputation: take defence, prosecution and civil cases (prepare, rely on your closing, or bend the rules), cross-examine witnesses (played), research precedent, court clients and work pro bono. Judges need Karma 50, and as a judge you hear cases and pass sentence instead. Big cases now count as wins and losses.
- Street hustler (Market Day) · Street rep: pick a pitch (high street, covered market or tourist square, each with its own crowd and fine risk), busk (played), sell from a stall, put on street shows and buy a trading licence. Festival season brings extra takings.
- Saves from before this update: a career already under way gets its Hub set up with a meter that fits the rank reached.

## "Calling": the looks come alive (13 Nov)
Every special career's Hub is now a set of its own (docs/phase-2/03, 6.3).
- Signature instruments replace the plain meter bar: a marquee sign whose bulbs light with Buzz, a VU meter whose needle rides your Following, a stadium scoreboard, a contact sheet with a frame for every ten Portfolio points, a hemicycle of a hundred seats lit by Approval (with the majority line), a stencilled dog tag and ammunition pips, the scales of justice tipped by Reputation, a patient monitor, an altimeter counting logbook hours, a hillside that fills with sheep, chalk tally marks on a slate, and a police badge with the light bar. All animate gently; Reduce motion holds them still.
- Illustrated scenes across the top of each Hub: velvet curtains and a roaming spotlight, a stage with sweeping beams over a bobbing crowd, a night stadium under floodlights, a runway lined with camera flashes, a chamber with a ballot slip dropping, a training area with a radar sweep, panelled chambers with law books and a green banker's lamp, a hospital corridor at night, the view from the flight deck, a valley whose sun follows the season, a market of striped awnings and bunting, and a city at night behind police tape.
- Each Hub speaks its trade: "Top billing / Call sheet / Press cuttings" for actors, "Chain of command / Orders / Service record" for the armed forces, "Seniority / Flight plan / Logbook" for pilots, and so on, in the look's own type. The ladder is a track with the career's emblem on your rung; the season strip marks the current beat; every career activity has its own icon.
- Your people: each special career's boss and colleagues become its own cast (an agent, a director who rates you and a rival actor; a consultant, a charge nurse and a rival registrar...). They live in People. A close ally puts in a word each year; a rival competes with you for the same role, seat or promotion: beat them fairly, undermine them, or back them and maybe win an ally.
- Lawyers get a 2am call when someone they know is arrested: defend them yourself, pay for the best, or stay out of it. Doctors see people they know come in by ambulance: treat them yourself against the rules, hand them to a colleague, or pull strings. Judges hear cases from the crime list, and sometimes the defendant is someone they know: step aside, or risk the inquiry.
- The Life Recap has a page for every special career you had, worn in its look: its scene, the title you reached, your best meter, highlights and its stamp. Highlights hide with "Hide names" or "Hide crimes".

## "Calling" step 6: Astronaut and Fighter (13 Nov)
- Astronaut (Orbit): needs a science or engineering degree, Health 70 and a pilot licence or Smarts 75. Train in the docking simulator (a new mini-game: centre the port and brake, with limited fuel), the centrifuge, survival training and experiments, and visit schools. Mission specialists fly to the space station (with a first spacewalk), commanders to the Moon, expedition leaders to Mars. Every launch shows its risk to life first; readiness lowers it, and you can always scrub. A launch can end a life.
- Fighter (Fight Night): choose boxing or MMA and fight in a fictional league. Train, spar, cut weight and promote your fights; fight with the new Fight Night mini-game (read the tell: Guard a jab, Slip a hook, Strike an opening, and watch your stamina). Build a record with knockouts, carry injuries until you rest, and win a title fight to become champion. Crews may offer money to throw a fight: taking it is fraud, with evidence and the risk of arrest; refusing can cost you a gym window.
- Street hustler scams: the three-card trick, a raffle with no prize, or a collection for a charity that does not exist, run through the crime system (evidence, cases, arrests). Getting caught costs your trading licence.
- Both new looks have their own instrument (a transfer chart from Earth to Mars with go/no-go lights; a gold title belt whose studs light with your ranking), an illustrated scene (Earth's limb with the station drifting by; the arena under the lights), section names, icons, stamps, people (the chief of the astronaut office, a crewmate, a rival for the seat; a head coach, a cornerman, a rival in your division) and Life Recap pages.

## "Calling: Encore" (14 Nov)
Finishing the special careers update to its full plan (docs/phase-2/03), and a youth unit for young offenders.
- Original mini-games for eleven careers, each its own design instead of a reworded general game: the cold read (actor: read the note under each line and pick the delivery), the soundcheck (musician: ride four faders as the room fills up), match day (athlete: find the open teammate or shoot), the runway (model: turn to the press pit, then pose to the brief), the debate (politician: read the room and pick the rebuttal), the obstacle course (armed forces: the right move for each obstacle, faster as you tire), cross-examination (lawyer: object to testimony that does not square with your notes, and to hearsay), triage (doctor: resus, majors or minors), diagnose (vet: match two signs to their cause with the handbook), the crosswind landing (pilot: crab into the gusts, then flare) and the pitch (street hustler: call out to people who are looking, never while an officer passes). All are turn-based with Reduce motion or a screen reader, and Skip still gives a fair result. The old games stay in the rotation for variety.
- Each career's motif runs along the top of the game while you work in it (marquee bulbs, VU meters, a heartbeat line, wood grain, a light bar…), like Gilded's seam and the prison fence.
- Careers reach into the rest of your life: a co-star romance (or an affair, if you are taken); a doping offer for athletes, with random tests and a two- or four-season ban; coalition offers for politicians, and bills written from this year's headline; malpractice claims for doctors after a bad night on the ward; and an investigation for pilots after an incident, where blaming maintenance can cost the licence.
- Second acts: when a special career ends (not by a conviction), you are offered what comes next: the athlete who coaches or becomes a pundit, the actor who directs, the model who designs, the pilot who instructs, and two for every special career. It goes on the career's Life Recap page.
- The youth unit: under-18s serve their time on a youth unit instead of an adult prison, with no gangs, riots, yard fights or contraband. Go to lessons (two years earn a school diploma), talk to your key worker, and meet the person you harmed, which can take a year off.
- Second acts can end into a third: step away from a role like Head Coach or TV Pundit and one more door can open (General Manager, Network Anchor...), the same way the first second act did. Every second act has its own next step; nothing comes after that.
- Adults can take a restorative justice meeting in prison, the grown-up counterpart to the youth unit's one. How it goes is a reason the parole board reads at your next hearing, shown with the rest (behaviour, programmes, family support).

## Season tabs you can play (21 Nov)
The four season tabs on every career page (Training · Exercises · Deployment · Leave for the armed forces, Write · Record · Release · Tour for musicians, and so on for all 14 special careers) were only a marker. Now each one is a choice:
- Once a year, tap a tab to make it the year's focus. A sheet says what it does before you commit, and the result pops up like any activity (it costs energy and goes in the feed).
- Each tab is one of four kinds: steady work (the meter always goes up), real work (up, more if it goes well), the big moment (Deployment, Premiere, Play-offs, Fight night, Trial, Launch window...: odds grow with the meter; a win jumps the meter, lifts performance, pays and counts a medal or title, a loss costs meter and a little health) and time off (Leave, Recovery, Off-season...: happiness and health come back, the meter slips).
- The highlighted tab is the season now and counts for more ("In season").
- A focus year is not a quiet year, so the meter does not fade on top.
- Musicians grow listeners and police close cases, since their meters come from those.

## Every career plays its own way (23 Nov)
The season tabs now open a sheet of choices that belong to the career, and every career has a signature mechanic of its own, drawn on its page:
- Armed forces: boot prints of fatigue fill with each course, route and posting and make the next one riskier; Leave clears them. Exercises is a route across the moor (road, ridge or bog); Deployment is a posting (peacekeeping, frontline, rescue), and the family misses you while you are away.
- Athlete: a Form vs Fatigue scoreboard with an injury zone and your last five results. Pick the training block, play every minute or rotate, take the decisive shot in the play-offs, then stay loyal, push for a transfer or sign a sponsor.
- Musician: a taped-up setlist. Write songs by mood (each rated in stars), record the newest in a bedroom, indie or major studio, pick how to drop it, then a tour route on a map; a better setlist plays better.
- Actor: a typecast film strip. Read for one of four scripts; three roles in one genre and you are typecast, so your type lands more often and the rest gets harder. Red-carpet looks set your reviews.
- Politician: a balance scale between the whip and your conscience, a budget bar to split, a recess and a campaign. Debates go best near the middle.
- Police: a personnel file stamped CLEAN or MARKED and an evidence board. Shortcuts close cases faster but mark your file.
- Doctor: a burnout heartbeat line. Triage four patients at once; over 80 burnout, mistakes happen; a holiday brings it down.
- Lawyer: a brass won–lost plaque and an ethics bar. Pick from three client files; trials and appeals go on the record; principled lawyers make partner more easily.
- Model: an image dial from wholesome to edgy and three magazine-cover campaign offers. Luxury houses want balance; controversial brands want edge.
- Vet: four farms with hearts of trust. At harvest you can only reach two; the others drift away.
- Pilot: a logbook of airport stamps. A full page of eight pays a bonus and a holiday. Winter ops is a storm on the radar: push on, hold or divert.
- Street hustler: a chalkboard of corners. Busy ones pay more, but the police move you on.
- Astronaut: a countdown ring. Selection and training count down to a crew; only then is GO open in the launch window.
- Fighter: a body damage chart and tonight's opponent. Strike beats grapple, grapple beats defend, defend beats strike.
Choices show what they move as chips (meter, stats, money, the signature, and how likely it is, in words). Many play a mini-game, and a good score raises the odds. Still once a year, and the tab in season still counts for more.
