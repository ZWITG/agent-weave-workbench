# 13 Client Pitch Script and FAQ

*Purpose:* The presenter's talk track for the 12-slide client pitch and prepared answers to the questions clients are most likely to ask.

> [!note] How to use this note
> A 12-slide, **~11-minute** talk track (10–12 minute window), followed by 20 likely client questions with crisp answers. Every number on a slide comes from [[01 Executive Summary]], [[02 Market and Competitors]] or [[03 Player Research and Wishlist]], where the sources are cited. **Say "estimate" or "target" out loud whenever a slide shows one.** The deck structure follows publisher and investor guidance: lead with the game, then proof, comps, model, budget and a clear ask; avoid unrealistic comps and math errors ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-2953); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/pitch-perfect-how-to-prepare-your-game-for-investors-and-publishers/); [Positech](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2015/01/06/how-to-pitch-your-indie-game-to-a-vc-investor/)).

> [!warning] Language guardrails for the presenter
> - Say "inspired by the text life-sim genre". Never say "BitLife clone", "BitLife 2" or imply any link with BitLife, Candywriter or Stillfront.
> - Name BitLife only as the market leader and a comparison point.
> - No screenshots of BitLife in the deck. Describe its UI in words or with neutral wireframes.
> - "Relivable" is a working title pending a trademark check.

## Likely client questions

### Strategy and positioning
**1. Why not just copy BitLife?**
Copying invites legal risk and inherits the exact things players resent: fragmented paywalls, ads, opaque mechanics. It also gives players no reason to switch from a game they already own. The value is in the redesign: memory and consequence, clearer UI, fair pricing and accessibility. We match the *genre's* systems (birth-to-death, careers, crime, generations), which are common to many games, with original writing, names and presentation.

**2. What's the legal risk of a game in the same genre as BitLife?**
Low if we stay disciplined, though it is not zero. Game mechanics and genres are generally not protected by copyright, but specific expression is: text, art, characters, UI layout and "look and feel". Courts have found infringement where a clone copied visual expression too closely (e.g. *Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive*, 2012). Our controls:
- All original text, event writing, names and assets.
- No BitLife-specific product terms (e.g. Bitizen, Bitizenship, Boss Mode) in the game or store metadata, and our own names for equivalent features.
- A distinct visual identity.
- IP counsel review before soft launch, plus a trademark check on "Relivable".

*This is general orientation, not legal advice. Budget for counsel is in [[11 Roadmap Team and Budget]].*

**3. Why would players leave a game they've played for years?**
Many already have: US weekly active users fell from a 2.78M peak (Dec 2023) to about 1.0–1.4M (Q2 2025) on Sensor Tower estimates. The pitch to them is concrete: every career free, four generations per dynasty free (up to Generation 8 with Plus), your purchases follow your account, and a story that remembers you. Lapsed players are our win-back persona ([[03 Player Research and Wishlist]]).

**4. What if the incumbent copies our features?**
Some features (recaps, percentages) are easy to copy. The fairness model is not, because matching it would cannibalise a catalogue of roughly ten paid unlocks and packs. Our deeper moat is a content engine built around memory from day one, plus a community and accessibility reputation that takes years to earn.

**5. Isn't the market too small?**
It's a niche, but a lucrative one. The leader alone is roughly a $40M-per-year franchise *(estimate)*, Life Simulator is among the top hybrid-casual subgenres by ARPU, and low art costs keep our break-even modest. We don't need to win the category; a single-digit share is a healthy business *(illustrative)*.

### Product and design
**6. Why text-first in 2026? Don't players want 3D?**
Text is the genre's core appeal, and it's what makes the outrageous, shareable moments cheap to produce at volume. We add an aging avatar, illustrated moments and home customisation for visual appeal without 3D cost or performance risk. The Sims Mobile's closure shows how expensive the full-visual route is.

**7. How do you avoid repetition, the genre's biggest gameplay complaint?**
Three mechanisms:
- **Storylets** with conditions, cooldowns and weights.
- **NPC memory flags**, so past choices unlock later events.
- A **simulator bot** that runs thousands of lives in CI to find events that appear too often or never.

We're planning 150 events at prototype, 1,500+ at MVP and thousands at launch, delivered remotely so content keeps flowing without app updates.

**8. What does "accessible by default" actually cost?**
Little, if it's done from day one. That's the point. Flutter generates an accessibility tree for standard widgets, and we add labels and semantics as we build. The cost is mainly QA time, which we treat as a release gate. Retrofitting later is what breaks; the incumbent's regressions show that.

### Business model
**9. How do you make money without paywalls?**
We do have one paywall. It's optional, one-time and honest: **Relivable Plus (~$9.99)** removes every ad and adds up to 8 generations, Destiny Mode and unlimited Rewind (a $2.99 No Ads option upgrades to Plus for $6.99). On top of that:
- **Opt-in rewarded ads**, capped at 4 per session and 8 per day. US rewarded eCPMs run about $12–20.
- A cosmetic and story **Season Pass** (~$4.99).
- **Story Packs** (~$2.99–3.99) that add storylines and never lock careers.
- Cosmetics: a $1.99 Welcome Pack and $0.99–1.99 sets. Two bundles of Plus and Story Packs at $14.99 and $24.99.
- No premium currency. Our only soft currency, Sparks, is earned in play and never sold.
- **Web-shop link-outs** from global launch (Phase 4) where allowed. The US Apple fee is currently 0%; Apple has proposed 15%, pending court approval.

Base case: ~$0.07 blended global ARPDAU (≥ $0.20 in Tier-1 markets) and 2–4% payers *(estimates to validate; see [[06 Monetization and Economy]])*.

**10. Won't one $9.99 unlock cap our revenue?**
It caps revenue from the *unlock*, not from the player. Seasons and Story Packs provide recurring spend, and rewarded ads monetise the 96%+ who never pay. A single clear unlock also converts better than a confusing ladder and earns the word of mouth that cuts acquisition cost. We'll A/B test price points in soft launch via remote config.

**11. Why no subscription?**
The genre's leader tested a subscription in 2019 and dropped it after a "nearly unanimously negative" reaction. A one-time unlock fits our "Fair forever" promise. We can revisit an optional VIP tier later if players ask for it.

### Technology and AI
**12. Why Flutter and not Unity?**
Our research found Unity the lowest-risk default for games in general. Relivable, though, is a UI-heavy text game, and Flutter wins on that profile:
- Best built-in VoiceOver/TalkBack support.
- Fast UI iteration.
- Cheaper hiring from the large app-developer pool.
- Shorebird for over-the-air patches.

Rendering performance isn't the deciding factor for 2D layered avatars. Unity 6 remains our documented alternative if the client wants heavier 2D animation; the stay-or-switch decision is made at the end of Phase 2 against the spike criteria. See [[08 Technical Architecture]].

**13. What if AI backfires?**
We designed for that. AI is **optional, off by default, on-device first, and only rewords text**. It never decides outcomes, so the game is fully playable and balanced without it. Every output passes moderation, with an in-app report button, as Google Play requires. We don't headline AI in marketing; we headline memory, which the deterministic engine delivers on its own. The inZOI backlash and the Peridot shutdown are our cautionary examples. Worst case, we switch AI off remotely with no gameplay loss.

**14. What does AI cost us?**
On-device (Apple Foundation Models) costs nothing per call. Cloud fallback on a small model is about $0.27 per active user per month at our assumed usage *(estimate)*. That's why we cache it and gate the cloud version to premium players.

**15. How do you stop lost saves and purchases?**
- Purchases are tied to an account and verified server-side (RevenueCat with StoreKit 2 and Play verification).
- Saves sync to the cloud through Firebase.
- A new phone, a reinstall or an update restores everything.

This is a top complaint in the genre, and it's a solved engineering problem.

### Compliance and risk
**16. Is this game safe for kids? What's the rating?**
It isn't a kids' game. Crime, drugs and romance put it at an expected **Apple 16+ / Google Mature 17+**, comparable to the genre leader. We use the platform age-signal APIs required by 2026 US state laws (Texas, Utah, Louisiana), block under-13s, and apply high-privacy defaults for teens. A softened teen mode is a client decision with a content cost. See [[09 Compliance and Ratings]].

**17. Do loot-box laws affect us?**
No. We have no paid random rewards anywhere. Real-money prices are always shown, and in-game gambling is simulated with no cash-out. That keeps us clear of Belgium's and Brazil's rules and ahead of the EU Digital Fairness Act.

### Delivery
**18. What's the biggest risk to the timeline?**
Writing throughput. Thousands of high-quality events is the critical path, not engineering. We mitigate it with:
- A data-driven authoring pipeline, with a validator and simulator.
- A writer-heavy team.
- Remote content delivery, so launch scope can grow after release.

**19. What happens if the prototype doesn't work?**
That's what Phase 2 is for. For about $25–60K and 8 weeks, you get real playtest data on fun, retention signals and UX before committing to the MVP budget. The go/no-go criteria are agreed up front ([[12 Risks and KPIs]]).

**20. How do you acquire users if paid UA is expensive?**
We lead with organic: shareable recaps, scenario seeds, creators and an official Discord. We soft-launch in cheaper markets, then scale paid UA only where LTV exceeds CPI. Casual iOS CPI averages about $1.41 globally, and US casual CPIs are much higher. We plan payback at D90–D180, and UGC-style creative raises install conversion (IPM) by about 152% on average ([Liftoff](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/liftoff-s-2025-mobile-ad-creative-index-reveals-ugc-is-a-key-differentiator-for-top-apps-837345804.html)).
